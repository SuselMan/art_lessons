# Helmholtz contact projection: CPU-only decision

Used real kept-contact flow fields with pad4, first/middle/last contacts of9/3 and8/1. Projection is the finite-volume graph Hodge projection: one shared edge flow per pair, solve graph Laplacian pressure with closed zero-throughflow boundary, subtract pair pressure gradient. CG converged relative residual <1.3e-8. Physical cell width4px omitted from divergence/curl units consistently; reported norms are grid-normalized, not per-worldpixel.

Full mean-velocity projection retains norm fractions9 .603/.393/.635 and8 .495/.526/.331. Dose-weighted projection retains9 .630/.499/.670 and8 .538/.495/.387. Weak25% projection leaves roughly .78–.87 of original norm, but that alone does not distinguish suppression of artificial compression from suppression of desired transport.

Critical counterexample: uniform translation v=(1,0) on closed rectangular contact domain is exactly grad(x); closed-boundary projection removes it entirely. The remaining curl for straight8 is largely the spatial window/nib edge, not actual brush rotation. Its norm cannot be interpreted as desirable circular paint motion. Closed projection enforces no-throughflow and thereby substitutes a local recirculation mechanism for the brush's actual translational push.

Projecting dose-weighted B*v is conceptually closer to flux, but B unchanged plus recovery vProjected/B is singular at zero/low-B border. Clamping velocity to unit norm then destroys the graph's exact divergence-free property. Projecting conditional velocity alone ignores B gradients: divergence(B*vProjected)=grad(B) dot vProjected even if div(vProjected)=0, so it does not eliminate contact-dose compression.

Alternatives also confounded: periodic boundaries retain mean translation but couple unrelated opposite nib edges; homogeneous zero-pressure normal derivative preserves translation only by allowing original boundary flux, leaving dose-window compression at the boundary. A global throughflow-preserving velocity decomposition subtracting mean before projection keeps brush translation but boundary conditions and reconciling with unchanged B remain constitutive choices, not proven corrections. Partial projection25% principally attenuates irrotational motion including true translation.

Decision: no runtime/GPU spec from this closed projection. It is mathematically valid incompressibility operator but mismatched to localized translating brush footprint and existing carrier model. A credible next design would project a genuinely free-fluid velocity over the whole receiving wet domain with explicit open boundary throughflow, rather than per-contact masked conditional velocity. That exceeds a narrow no-new-state experiment. No claim that compressions/washboard are physically correct: only this proposed repair is not sufficiently isolated.
