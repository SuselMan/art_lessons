Teach on the drawing, not through screen sharing.

Grafetto is one sheet of paper for you and your student. You both draw on it at the same time, from anywhere, with pencils that respond to pressure and tilt the way graphite does.

How a lesson goes

  1. Open a room

    One click gives you a blank sheet and a link. Put a reference photo on its own layer if the task needs one.

  2. Send the link

    Your student opens it in a browser on a tablet or a computer with a pen tablet. No account, no install.

  3. Draw together

    You see every stroke as it is made. Correct the drawing on its own layer, then hide it so the student can try again.

  4. Leave homework

    The room stays after the lesson. The student keeps working in it, and you look at the result before the next one.

Everyone has their own view: zoom in and rotate the sheet like paper on a desk, without moving it for the other person.

Materials that behave like materials

Paper has tooth, and every tool reacts to it. Pressure makes a line darker, tilt makes it broader, and graphite builds up in layers instead of flattening into one colour.

  • Graphite pencil

    Fourteen grades from 6H to 6B, plus colour.

  • Charcoal

    Vine, willow and compressed — each crumbles and catches the paper differently.

  • Liner and brush pen

    An even ink line for sketching and architecture, a swelling one for manga and lettering.

  • Marker

    Bullet and chisel tips; overlapping passes get darker, like alcohol markers do.

  • Watercolour

    Washes pool on the paper and dry with a darker edge.

  • Digital brush

    For lessons in digital drawing — soft and hard brushes that stay out of the way.

Also on the table: an eraser, a blending stump, a ruler, a proportion grid and layers.

The lesson doesn't end when the call does

Every room is kept. A student comes back to the same sheet between lessons, and you see what they did — the drawing itself, not a phone photo of it. Close a room when the work is done, and it stays readable for both of you.

Works where you already teach

iPad with Apple Pencil, Android tablets with a stylus, and computers with a Wacom or similar pen tablet. Keep your usual video call running alongside — Grafetto is the paper, not the camera.
